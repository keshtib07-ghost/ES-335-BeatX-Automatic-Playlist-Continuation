01 / How it works

Your songs supply the starting point.

Three seeds seen in training

  1. Find related songs. Item-based collaborative filtering (CF) finds songs that shared training playlists with your seeds. Cosine similarity adjusts for popularity; shrinkage reduces trust in rare pairs.
  2. Combine the evidence. Add neighbour scores across seeds, excluding the seeds themselves. Each seed contributes up to 200 neighbours.
  3. Use context and filters. Title rules and a text classifier infer tags. Song-tag associations provide context; selected tags, BPM and era constrain eligibility. A configured context boost can adjust the CF order.
  4. Return the playlist. Return up to 20 additions, with fewer when evidence or filters limit the options. Playback flow can reorder them; Spotify export saves them.

Some seeds are unfamiliar

Use any seeds with training evidence. Unknown seeds add no learned similarity. If none is usable, rank popular training songs using the title and selected context. If the title is vague too, fall back to training popularity, subject to active filters.

Recognized tracks supply similarity evidence. Explicit filters control eligibility, and candidate tracks come from the training catalogue.

02 / What we learned from

One million existing playlists.

Spotify’s Million Playlist Dataset provides song identities, titles and playlist membership—not trusted audio mood, BPM or bass measurements.

Training learns relationships; validation chooses settings; test evaluates frozen choices. We split playlists, so a song can occur in both training and test. Repeated track IDs within a playlist are removed, and exact track-set duplicates share a split.

We use all 804,485 training playlists for collaborative filtering and all their titles to fit both text classifiers. The standard classifier uses 12 tags. Studio’s expanded classifier uses 19 tags and scores the training split to rebuild song-context associations. Training details →

Title context adds +0.00160 NDCG@20 overall (paired 95% CI 0.00107 to 0.00212). The classifier assigned tags to 657 of 5,000 titles.

More seeds helped in a separate study.

The earlier seed-count experiment compares 1, 2, 3, 5, 10 and 20 seeds on 1,500 validation and 1,500 fresh test queries, keeping each query’s references fixed. Learned-title test NDCG rises from 0.1284 to 0.3142.

A validation-only rule selects 20 seeds as the smallest tested budget reaching 95% of peak validation NDCG. This is the largest budget tested; we did not establish an early plateau or a universal optimum.

Context did not always help.

Keyword and learned-title rankings were close in the earlier seed-count study. At 20 seeds, title reranking improves 84 queries, worsens 70 and leaves 1,346 unchanged. The tagged-query interval includes zero.

Recovery can improve while catalogue coverage falls. Artist variety, catalogue reach and popularity bias describe different tradeoffs; none directly measures listening satisfaction.

The seed-count study used its own fitted tagger, query cohort and fixed references. The live Studio uses the rebuilt 19-tag profiles with extra filtering and ranking logic. Its relevance needs a separate evaluation.

What else did we measure?

The full-title benchmark’s tagged-query NDCG rises from 0.20355 to 0.21572. The report also includes Precision@20, hit rate, paired bootstrap intervals, marginal seed gains and actual diagnostic cases. B reserves 20 possible seeds; all other tracks form the fixed references. Unrevealed reserved seeds earn no recovery credit.

A separate order study holds the same 20 known songs and first song fixed. Training-transition ordering recovers 15.61% of original directed adjacencies versus 5.57% for a seeded shuffle, across 401 eligible sequences. 04 / What these results do not establish

Useful clues, with limits.

  • Tags describe grouping. A Gym tag reflects workout-like playlist titles. Listeners did not verify workout suitability; titles can mislead, and sparse evidence may leave songs untagged.
  • Unseen tracks remain a weakness. In the full-title test, 5,922 of 316,437 withheld occurrences (1.87%) are absent from training. They stay in the scoring denominator. Seven queries have all three seeds unseen.
  • Offline recovery is only one test. Existing playlists are not a complete list of good recommendations or explicit dislikes. Exact Spotify IDs can also separate alternate releases of the same recording.
  • New features need their own evidence. The deployed Studio loads the expanded profiles. Controlled comparisons would be needed to measure the ranking effects of the expanded profile logic, BPM, era, bass and treble.

The next evaluations can use human-reviewed title labels, measure candidate recall and isolate the contribution of the expanded profiles.
