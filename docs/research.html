What counts as a prediction?

One query is a held-out playlist. The user supplies visible seed songs and optional context. The model returns up to 20 additions. Hidden songs are used only to score the result.

Data unit
One playlist; each matrix entry records whether a song belongs to it.
Prediction time
After the request, before new songs are selected.
Available information
The request and frozen training evidence.
Claim
Recovery of hidden songs. Listening satisfaction is not measured.

01 / How it works

How the final pipeline works

When the model recognises your seeds

  1. Find related songs. Collaborative filtering (CF) finds songs that appeared in playlists with your seeds. It adjusts for song popularity and gives less weight to pairs seen together only a few times.
  2. Combine the seed scores. Each seed supplies up to 200 related songs. We add their scores and exclude the seeds from the new recommendations.
  3. Apply context. The title and seed profiles suggest context tags. Your selected filters determine which songs are allowed. A configured context weight can also change their order.
  4. Return the results. Recommend up to 20 additions. Strict filters can produce fewer. Playback flow changes their order; Spotify export puts your seeds first, followed by the recommendations.

When seeds are missing from training

Use the seeds the model knows. If it knows none, use the title and selected filters to rank popular songs from training. If the title yields no usable context either, use popularity alone. Active filters still apply.

The model cannot learn the sound of an unfamiliar seed from its name. Its recommendations come from songs seen during training.

02 / Dataset

Data and training split

Spotify’s Million Playlist Dataset records which songs appeared in each playlist. It includes titles and track metadata. Verified mood labels and audio measurements must come from other sources.

We learn from the training playlists, choose settings on validation, then evaluate the fixed model on test playlists. A song may appear in more than one split. Repeated track IDs within a playlist count once, and playlists containing exactly the same track set stay together.

CF uses all 804,485 training playlists. Both text classifiers use all their titles. The benchmark classifier predicts 12 tags; Studio predicts 19 and rebuilds each song’s context profile from the training playlists. Training details →

04 / Limitations

What the results do not show

  • Tags come from titles. A Gym tag means a song appeared in playlists with workout-related titles. Listeners have not verified its suitability for exercise. Vague titles and limited evidence can produce incorrect or missing tags.
  • Unseen songs cannot be recovered. In the three-seed test, 5,922 of 316,437 hidden song occurrences (1.87%) were absent from training. They still count when we calculate recall. Seven playlists had no seed seen during training.
  • A missed match is not necessarily a bad recommendation. An existing playlist cannot list every suitable song. Alternate releases may also have different Spotify IDs, even when they contain the same recording.
  • Extra features do not automatically improve recovery. The final Studio comparison finds no clear average NDCG gain over CF. The tested BPM filter lowers recovery. Release-date filtering and bass features still need separate comparisons.
